The Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) has been entrusted with stewardship of four additional AI projects in the hope of broadening the set of vendor-neutral tools for making AI agents. The AAIF was founded in December 2025 under the auspices of the Linux Foundation as a vendor-neutral organization for the benefit of AI companies more interested in riding a tide that lifts all boats than making waves. It launched with three projects under its supervision: Model Context Protocol (MCP), goose, and AGENTS.

With the four new adoptees, there are now 10 projects sheltering under the AAIF's wings. The donor organizations – Alibaba, Anthropic, Hugging Face, and OpenAI – have handed over control of OpenSandbox, Agent Skills, OpenEnv, and Open Responses respectively. Each of these projects provides functions that developers of AI agents – which typically combine models, tools, and a task loop – may find helpful.

And having a more or less agreed-upon blueprint for implementation tends to make deployment easier and more predictable, at least until your AI agent unexpectedly ransacks some third-party organization. OpenSandbox provides a way to constrain or sandbox the capabilities of AI agents using various client SDKs, tools, protocols, lifecycle hooks, container runtimes, and assorted in-sandbox execution components. Agent Skills outlines a format for text descriptions that guide AI agents through specified tasks more effectively.

In a world full of cyber risk, account takeover has emerged as one of the most insidious of all threats. If an attacker can successfully compromise a legitimate email account, they are then able to pose as a trusted individual or organization without triggering any warnings. Traditional email security will see nothing wrong.

From there the threat actor can pick their moment to cause havoc with objectives that range from stealing money, expropriating sensitive data and propagating catastrophic malware. Serious financial loss and a damaged corporate reputation are commonplace outcomes. The typical account takeover lifecycle starts with the bad guys obtaining credentials, usually through the medium of phishing.

Generative AI (GenAI) has made it easy for an attacker to trick an unwary individual into giving away login data, perhaps by posing as a colleague or other trusted party or by encouraging them to enter confidential information on a fake login page. You might expect, once a legitimate identity has been successfully compromised, that all hell would immediately break loose. These days the typical account takeover artist is more stealthy and sinister than that, in no rush to act at this early stage since no alarm bells have been triggered.
