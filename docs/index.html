Just 403 hyperscale datacenters consumed an estimated 1. 8 percent of US electricity in 2024-25, and their carbon dioxide emissions were roughly two to three times an earlier forecast for 2018. Research led by the Harvard T.

Chan School of Public Health and the Harvard Data Science Initiative looked specifically at hyperscale facilities, the largest class of server farms that typically support cloud computing and AI processing. The published study, " Assessing the carbon emissions and energy consumption of US hyperscale data centres ," estimates their combined electricity use approached that of a single state such as Indiana, Michigan, or Tennessee under the highest-load scenario. The researchers identified 403 hyperscale facilities by using a larger datacenter dataset, satellite imagery, and building footprints on OpenStreetMap.

Rather than reading electricity meters, the researchers worked out consumption from each site's electrical capacity, the hours in a year, and assumptions about how heavily the facility was running. Across four operating scenarios, ranging from low-intensity use to AI-heavy workloads, electricity draw was believed to total 68-99 terawatt-hours (TWh). Each of those states consumed approximately 100 TWh during the study period.
