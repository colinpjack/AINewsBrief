1Password is reassuring customers that their vaults remain secure after an attacker breached its systems and stole account information and private source code. The Canadian security biz disclosed this week that an attacker had compromised an employee's corporate GitHub account, made off with private source code, and deployed malicious code to its backend services to siphon off customer information. 1Password, which began investigating the intrusion on November 19, hasn't said how many customers were affected, but confirmed that the stolen data includes names and email addresses used to sign in to the service, account identifiers, timestamps showing when users created and last accessed their accounts, and various pieces of non-secret account metadata.

The company insists the information that customers actually pay it to protect was beyond the intruder's reach. "At no time were customer vault contents accessed by, or available to, the attacker," 1Password said. Arguably more troubling is the revelation that the intruder gained access to 1Password's continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD) infrastructure, which houses credentials used to keep its products running.

The company says it has rotated or revoked potentially exposed credentials and that neither its apps nor its browser extensions were affected. 1Password hasn't explained how access to an employee's GitHub account enabled the attacker to deploy malicious code to its backend services, nor has it detailed the extent of the attacker's access to its software build and distribution infrastructure. The company did not respond to The Register's questions.
