The image of the red ring around the black hole M87, produced by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) array, made front-page news around the world in 2019. There was one problem: there was a slim chance the depiction might be wrong. This week, a small team of four Australian researchers have published their work confirming the result based on a new machine learning algorithm using the same base data.

The 2019 Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) image came from hundreds of scientists working together to capture the first direct view of a black hole's shadow. The measurements were taken at eight telescopes around the world, at locations including Chile, the US, and Antarctica. Reconstructing the image from the data so geographically distant meant instrument and environment errors could influence the accuracy of the resulting image.

Potentially, alternative analyses of the data could have reconstructed an image without the iconic ring shape. Samuel Lai – a postdoctoral fellow at the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO) in Western Australia – and his team created an independent imaging algorithm to process the historic dataset to validate the result. The new approach used closed triplets (using three telescopes for error correction) rather than station pairs in an effort to reduce error.
