For the past month, we’ve been inundated with Black Friday deals, signaling what’s to come on the biggest shopping day of the year in the US. We routinely get dozens of press releases a day, coming from brands big and small that hope that we’ll publicize these offers. Of course, the companies would prefer that we don’t do any research on these deals to see if they’re actually good, and most don’t provide any of the necessary context we’re looking for when vetting them.

Most indicators of a good deal are confusing — not just for us, but for anyone who’s shopping. Groundwork Collaborative recently published its findings about the unclear manner in which Amazon displays deals. Its researchers tracked a selection of products ahead of Black Friday and Cyber Monday in 2024 and 2025, and they claim that nearly one in three products advertised as deals on Amazon didn’t offer savings during the actual shopping events.

The investigation also found that nearly a tenth of the deals it examined were more expensive during the sales events than on some days in the month leading up to them. Something we commonly see on Amazon and attempt to correct for in our coverage are good deals with inflated discount claims. As in, the original retail amount (the “list” price) is sometimes higher than the usual day-to-day price to make the savings seem more substantial.
