However, it alluded to open source AI security tools that were initially intended to assist security researchers being repurposed for nefarious activity. Attackers are already repurposing such tools. For example, researchers are increasingly seeing widespread abuse of ARTEX, an open source, Chinese-made AI red-teaming platform, in attacks on UK registrar 123 Reg , Microsoft 365 , and others since its launch in July.

In its pessimistic scenario for 2028, the NCSC envisages "a significant increase in the frequency and sophistication of cyberattacks worldwide," with wider adoption of AI tools making attribution harder. China "has likely played a role" in supplying those tools in this future, "enabling them to hide their activities within the noise," the report states. Not wanting these vibey biases to seep into the realm of AI, Lawson created a benchmark to compare how different JavaScript frameworks perform under the same agentic model. The results are illuminating. In general, agents tend to work more efficiently with server-centric frameworks than with client-heavy “Single-Page App” (SPA) frameworks (which should tickle for React haters ). But the biggest take-away should be less controversial: agents best utilize those frameworks that favor convention, API stability, and concision.
