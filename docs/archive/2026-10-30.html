The report was commissioned by IT services biz Bytes Technology Group .

One reason for the lack of results is that organizations have invested heavily up front on retraining staff, reorganizing the way they work and other adjustments. Those costs show up straight away, yet the payback doesn’t come until later. Another reason, the report suggests, is that AI tools can raise productivity at the level of individual tasks, but these gains do not automatically translate into greater productivity across a worker’s whole role or the entire organization.

It cites the example of software developers using GitHub Copilot who completed around 26 percent more tasks, and professionals using ChatGPT for routine writing and analysis that reduced task completion time by 40 percent while improving the quality of the output by 18 percent. However, a study of AI dev tools found these have increased coding activity by approximately 240 percent, while the number of actual software releases has only grown by about 30 percent. The gap is due to humans still needing to check and test their work before release.
