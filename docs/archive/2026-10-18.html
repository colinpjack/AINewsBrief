Not all digital intrusions remain in cyberspace, and attackers don’t need to encrypt files and steal data to cause chaos. Hitting physical systems often produces more severe consequences - and turns up the heat on executives to pay an extortion demand.

“There was one gig where the customer was refusing to pay, and so the threat actor was like, ‘Hold my beer, have fun getting into your offices,’ and locked everybody out,” Tim Beasley, a senior incident response consultant at Semperis, told The Register. Beasley won’t name the customer, or provide any incriminating information, but said the incident response involved police smashing glass in addition to his team recovering compromised data. “The cops had to break in, and literally get into the building, breaking some windows, so the customer was able to finally get back in,” he said.

One new gizmo, Bee Frontier, is designed to be worn like a watch or one of those fitness trackers, although to our mind it looks more like you are wearing a doorbell. The other, Bee Eclipse, is a slimline and lightweight device about the size of a credit card that attaches to the back of your phone magnetically, like a limpet. Both are intended to listen in to your conversations and help you remember important details, like when you are meeting your friend for a pint later and at which hospitality outlet.

The devices take note of the details you might otherwise forget, and then turn them into useful conversation summaries, to-dos and reminders, Amazon says. The pair of devices feature a single button to turn on or off, and a visible indicator that lights up when one is listening in and its AI is summarizing the conversation.
