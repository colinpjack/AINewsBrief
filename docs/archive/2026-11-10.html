AMD itself relies heavily on its X-series Epycs for EDA workloads like functional verification testing, which is used to simulate silicon in order to ensure it behaves as it's intended to.

According to AMD Fellow Phil Steinke, by keeping more of the model in cache, the CPU cores don't have to wait as long for data to move in and out of system memory. Normally, boosting L3 capacity would have required larger chiplets with lower yields, but that's not how AMD has gone about it for the past four years. Instead, it used advanced packaging to give its existing CPU dies a little SRAM backpack.

They did so using AlphaProof Code, a multi-agent framework for formal software specification and verification. AlphaProof Code relies on a human-guided agent that writes a Lean specification, assesses machine code semantics, and crafts an agent-authored proof that the Lean kernel checks against the specification.

In this instance, AlphaProof Code verified Signal’s post-quantum PQXDH handshake and Triple Ratchet , thereby validating the design of libsignal 's forward secrecy, authentication, post-compromise security, and handshake secrecy. In so doing, the Google/DeepMind team – Swarat Chaudhuri, Andrew Ferraiuolo, Anton Kovsharov, Tej Chajed, Sam Schlesinger, Karn Seth, Jonathan Katz, Charles Sutton, and Pushmeet Kohli – has taken a step toward a challenge the Beneficial AI Foundation issued in April to formally verify the Signal protocol and Signal app with Lean. The AlphaProof Code process involves working with various models to turn Signal's security goals into a human-reviewed mathematical specification.

Based on this spec, the researchers assigned Gemini Carbon agents to generate Lean code proving the correctness of the libsignal implementation. After the agents generated 6. 5 million lines of Lean code, they undertook an auditing pass and flagged bits that required human review.

Impervious to battlefield jamming, drones physically connected to human operators by miles of fiber-optic cables have become such a common weapon of war in Ukraine that trees and fields are strewn with tangled cables thinner than fishing lines. But importing such fiber-optic material from China has become more costly as drone warfare’s needs compete with global demand from AI data centers for the same limited supply—and so Ukrainian drone makers have been adapting. Since the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine began in February 2022, the Ukrainians have been forced out of necessity to continually develop battlefield innovations involving first-person view (FPV) quadcopter drones and other heavier types of drones.

But it was the Russian military that first deployed unjammable fiber-optic drones to repel Ukrainian forces’ cross-border incursion into the Kursk Oblast in 2024. The Ukrainians quickly followed suit in developing their own fiber-optic drones, given the drones’ capability to shrug off electronic warfare jamming that could neutralize radio-controlled drones. Russia and Ukraine have relied on Chinese suppliers for some off-the-shelf drones and a wide array of drone components, including bulk orders of fiber wound around very large spools or sometimes small finished spools ready for installation on drones.
