A datacenter's cooling architecture can have a decisive effect on its water and energy use, according to Schneider Electric, which argues that operators should evaluate it early in the design process. In a white paper published today, the energy management specialist compares four modeled 100 MW datacenter designs, ranging from traditional air cooling to optimized liquid-cooling systems. The paper, "Water Usage at AI Scale: Insights from a 100 MW Comparative Analysis," concludes that moving from air to liquid cooling consistently improves energy efficiency and can reduce water consumption in constrained locations.

Schneider has a commercial interest in that conclusion. It acquired a 75 percent controlling stake in liquid-cooling specialist Motivair in 2024 and said it intended to buy the remainder by 2028. The assessment also models the effect of location by applying the four designs to the annual weather profiles of Paris, France, and Dallas, Texas.

Schneider's model puts the highest onsite water consumption at a traditionally air-cooled facility in Dallas. Switching to liquid cooling with coolant supplied at 45°C (113°F) reduces water use by at least 50 percent, it claims. The firm says the results demonstrate how location, operating temperature, and cooling architecture determine a facility's water requirements, even as rack densities and the demands of power-hungry AI systems increase.

Trump regards it as one of the European levies that create unfair trade barriers for US companies and has threatened the UK with a "big tariff" if it keeps the DST.

Introduced in 2020, the DST applies a 2 percent tax to certain revenues from UK users of search engines, social media services, and online marketplaces. It applies to groups generating more than £500 million globally from those activities and more than £25 million from UK users, with the first £25 million of UK revenue exempt. The tax was introduced to address what the government described as a mismatch between where digital businesses generate value and where their profits are taxed.

It is separate from corporation tax rather than a replacement for it. In their letter, signatories including Tax Justice UK, the National Education Union, 38 Degrees, Global Witness, and Cost of Living Action press the government to keep the DST and extend it to AI companies and other digital services. They also call for measures to prevent multinationals from shifting profits through low-tax jurisdictions.
