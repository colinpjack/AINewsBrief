"Datacenters are essentially several thousand computers, as are supercomputers.

"So, we essentially had the same problems," he says, especially as regards cooling and power management. There's no single formal route into supercomputer administration in Australia, O'Shea says. He picked up the necessary skills over time.

Today, I’m talking with Hayden Field, The Verge ’s senior AI reporter, about the Anthropic IPO for the third and final episode in our series about AI in real life. The AI lab behind Claude went public on the Nasdaq last Thursday. The numbers are gigantic, but not really that important for this conversation, except to say that Anthropic has become one of the most valuable companies on the planet.

The real story is what happens now — and whether Anthropic can continue to thread the needle of caring about and investing in AI safety while also being a public company beholden to shareholders. Oh, and it also continuously growing the amount of money it makes so it can keep paying its ever-increasing compute bills, which total roughly $900 billion over the coming years. All of these ideas sit in a fascinating tension with one another, and so Hayden and I really tried to break down how we think this might play out as Anthropic navigates the pressures of the public markets.

You’ll hear us talk about the company’s unique corporate governance structure , something that is especially relevant in the wake of the ongoing Jensen Huang saga. We also get into why OpenAI seems happy to have let Anthropic go public first , and why Anthropic’s whole pitch to big customers boils down to the same safety branding that now sits in conflict with the profit-oriented part of its business.
